Credibility on Demand

Checking the quality of AI-written answers by hand is slow and expensive, so more and more often another AI does the checking. But AI judges can be fooled, just like people. One example is authority bias: an answer seems better simply because it cites a source, whether or not the source is real. For my thesis I am building the first German dataset to measure this, and I test whether AI judges fall for it.

Recently I started using agentic AI to see whether I could make apps myself, and found I could turn ideas into finished apps quickly. Soon I had lots of ideas for apps that are cute and useful at the same time, and I wanted to build them under my own brand. Its name comes from ankabut, the Arabic word for spider, so a little spider climbs into every app icon.

Go Girls* Timer

For the Go Girls* Game Nights I wanted a Go clock in a style that fits the event: as cute and girly as the evenings themselves. I also needed one to practise for a tournament. So I built it with Claude Code, from idea to a first working version in one day. It lies on a phone next to the board, has Fischer and byo-yomi time, statistics with a chart of the time per move and several colour themes to switch between. It runs in the browser and will soon be available on Google Play.

Timebits

Most timers show a number; I wanted to see at a glance how much time is left. In Timebits every minute is a small item on the screen that leaves when its minute is over, so the picture gets visibly emptier as time passes. Tap into the empty space and it switches to another look.
